# Common Technical Indicators Explained

Technical indicators are mathematical calculations based on price, volume, or open interest data. They help traders identify trends, momentum, and potential entry/exit points.

## Moving Averages

**Simple Moving Average (SMA):** The average closing price over N periods. A 50-day SMA smooths out short-term noise to reveal the underlying trend.

**Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** Similar to SMA but gives more weight to recent prices. Reacts faster to price changes. Common periods: 12, 26, 50, 200 days.

## RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Calculated as: RSI = 100 - (100 / (1 + RS)), where RS = average gain / average loss over N periods (typically 14). Values range 0-100. Below 30 = oversold, above 70 = overbought.

## MACD

MACD Line = 12-period EMA minus 26-period EMA. Signal Line = 9-period EMA of MACD Line. When MACD crosses above the signal line, it's a bullish signal.

## Bollinger Bands

Middle band = 20-day SMA. Upper band = middle + 2 standard deviations. Lower band = middle - 2 standard deviations. Price outside the bands suggests extreme conditions.

## Volume

Rising prices with increasing volume confirms a trend. Rising prices with declining volume suggests the trend may be weakening.

## Choosing Indicators

For trend-following strategies, use moving averages and MACD. For mean reversion, use RSI and Bollinger Bands. Never rely on a single indicator. Combine 2-3 for confirmation.
