# Momentum Strategies

Momentum investing is based on the idea that assets which have performed well recently will continue to perform well in the near future, and assets that have performed poorly will continue to underperform.

## How Momentum Works

Momentum strategies measure the rate of change of an asset's price over a given lookback period. If a stock has risen significantly over the last 3-12 months, a momentum strategy would buy it, expecting the trend to continue.

## Common Momentum Indicators

**Rate of Change (ROC):** Measures the percentage change in price over N periods. A positive ROC indicates upward momentum.

**Moving Average Crossover:** When a short-term moving average (e.g. 20-day) crosses above a long-term moving average (e.g. 50-day), it signals bullish momentum. The reverse signals bearish momentum.

**MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence):** Calculated as the difference between a 12-period and 26-period exponential moving average. A MACD line crossing above its signal line is a buy signal.

## Simple Momentum Strategy Example

Entry rule: Buy when the 20-day SMA crosses above the 50-day SMA.
Exit rule: Sell when the 20-day SMA crosses below the 50-day SMA.
Stop loss: 5% below entry price.
Typical lookback: 6-12 months of historical data for parameter tuning.

## Risks

Momentum strategies can suffer from sudden reversals (momentum crashes). They work best in trending markets and struggle in sideways or choppy conditions.
