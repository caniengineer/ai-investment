# Risk Management Basics

Risk management is the most important part of any trading strategy. A great entry signal is worthless without proper risk controls.

## Position Sizing

Never risk more than 1-2% of your portfolio on a single trade. If your portfolio is $100,000 and your stop loss is 5% below entry, your maximum position size should be $20,000-$40,000.

## Stop Losses

A stop loss is a predetermined price at which you exit a losing trade. Types of stop losses:

**Fixed percentage:** Exit if the price drops X% below entry (common: 3-5%).
**ATR-based:** Exit if price moves more than N times the Average True Range against you. More adaptive to volatility.
**Trailing stop:** Moves up with the price but never moves down. Locks in profits while giving the trade room to breathe.

## Key Risk Metrics

**Sharpe Ratio:** Measures risk-adjusted return. Sharpe = (return - risk_free_rate) / standard_deviation. A Sharpe above 1.0 is good, above 2.0 is excellent.

**Maximum Drawdown:** The largest peak-to-trough decline. Drawdowns above 25% are dangerous for most strategies. Target max drawdown below 20%.

**Win Rate:** Percentage of trades that are profitable. A win rate above 50% combined with a positive risk/reward ratio is solid.

## Diversification

Don't put all capital into one asset or strategy. Spread across uncorrelated assets to reduce portfolio-level risk.

## Evaluation Thresholds

For a strategy to be considered viable: Sharpe ratio should be above 0.5, maximum drawdown should be below 25%, and win rate should be above 40%.
